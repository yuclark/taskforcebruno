## 1. Executive Summary & Research Framework

The Minimum Viable Product (MVP) validation of **Task Force Bruno: Integrated Campus Pet Management System** gathers empirical evidence regarding functional suitability, operational efficiency, user satisfaction, and institutional adoption feasibility across the **Cebu Institute of Technology – University (CIT-U)** ecosystem.

### 1.1 Selected Theoretical Frameworks

The research design synthesizes three established evaluation models:
1. **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM - Davis, 1989):** Evaluates *Perceived Usefulness (PU)*, *Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU)*, and *Behavioral Intention (BI)* to predict sustained adoption.
2. **System Usability Scale (SUS 4-Item Quick Benchmark - Brooke, 1996):** Measures cognitive complexity, user confidence, and learnability via alternating polarity statements.
3. **ISO 9241-11 & RA 9482 Compliance:** Evaluates objective technical capabilities (hardware QR camera decoding, AI search, multi-device viewport responsiveness) and regulatory animal welfare mandates (14-day bite/scratch observation quarantine tracking).

### 1.2 Framework Justification for Task Force Bruno

1. **Campus Stakeholder Representation:** The validation captures direct feedback from both key campus segments: CIT-U students (the primary mobile field reporters) and CIT-U faculty/subject matter experts (who evaluate educational suitability, institutional ethics, and campus biosecurity).
2. **Biosecurity Assurance:** Incorporating RA 9482 biosecurity compliance ensures the platform provides genuine safety protection to the university community.
3. **Hardware & Mobile Ergonomics:** Evaluating optical camera scanning and responsive layouts ensures high performance across diverse smartphone models.

The validation study targets an evaluation sample of **N = 34 campus participants** across the CIT-U ecosystem, classified according to the primary stakeholder roles established in Question 5 of the validation instrument:

| Target Stakeholder Role (Question 5) | User Segment | Primary Device (Question 4) | Instrument Scope & Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **CIT-U Faculty / Subject Matter Expert (SME)** | Institutional Stakeholders & Advisers | Desktop Browser / Smartphone | Institutional utility, biosecurity compliance (RA 9482 quarantine alerts), administrative workflows, and adoption governance. |
| **Total Target Sample Size** | **N = 34 Campus Respondents across Mobile & Desktop Platforms** | | |

> **Staff & Clinic Module Evaluation Protocol:** While the primary respondent demographic is categorized into Students and Faculty SMEs, specialized administrative scenarios (Section 6: Staff & Clinic Operational Modules, including 1-click sighting intake handoffs, physical unit supply logistics, and QR collar tag generation) are evaluated by designated faculty evaluators, clinic coordinators, and student welfare officers who perform administrative task walk-throughs.

## 4. Verification Task Protocols (Section 3 of Instrument)

Participants confirmed the specific scenarios executed during their evaluation session (Question 6 Checklist):

1. Registered an institutional account / Logged in to the portal
2. Scanned an animal QR tag or uploaded a QR code matrix using the Collar QR Scanner
3. Tested the AI Natural Language Trait Search (e.g., searching by coat color, tags, or location)
4. Submitted an Animal Sighting Report with location details and image upload
5. Navigated the Adoption Gallery and inspected animal profiles / immunization records
6. Tested responsive layout (switching or resizing between mobile and desktop viewports)
7. Tested Public Pet Passport without logging in (scanned/entered pet ID to view emergency ID and rabies status)
8. Observed distinction between Campus Resident Companions (permanent mascots) and Strays Available for Adoption
9. *(Staff Only)* Performed sighting triage and tested the "Admit & Intake Stray" transition button
10. *(Staff Only)* Generated and previewed a printable high-res Collar Tag QR code
11. *(Staff Only)* Inspected Supply Logistics inventory broken down by physical units (kg, cans, vials, assets)
12. *(Staff Only)* Tested the Administrative Dashboard (Sighting triage, medical logs, or inventory ledger)

## 5. Detailed Structured Questionnaire Alignment

### Section 4: Quantitative System Evaluation (5-Point Likert Scale + N/A)

#### Factor A: Perceived Usefulness (PU) & Functional Suitability
- **PU1 (Sighting Reporting):** The sighting submission form captures all critical details (photo, location landmark, physical state) needed to locate and rescue campus animals.
- **PU2 (Directory Transparency):** The system clearly differentiates between Permanent Campus Residents and Strays Available for Adoption, presenting vaccination badges and TNR status accurately.
- **PU3 (Campus Biosecurity & Rabies Quarantine):** The prominent active 14-day observation quarantine alerts for reported bite/scratch incidents provide critical safety protection for the CIT-U community in compliance with RA 9482.
- **PU4 (Institutional Coordination):** Task Force Bruno provides a significantly more structured, trackable coordination platform than unorganized Facebook groups or group chats.
- **PU5 (Overall Campus Utility):** Overall, the platform addresses real, urgent animal welfare and stray population management challenges across the CIT-U campus.

#### Factor B: Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) & Navigation
- **PEOU1 (Navigation & Layout):** The user interface hierarchy (navigation bars, cards, action buttons) is intuitive and easy to navigate without needing a user manual.
- **PEOU2 (Form Efficiency):** The input forms (sighting reports, adoption inquiries, profile views) are straightforward to complete with clear validation cues.
- **PEOU3 (Visual Coherence & Aesthetics):** The typography, official maroon/gold branding, responsive spacing, and SVG iconography feel clean, professional, and visually engaging.
- **PEOU4 (Session Continuity):** The system maintains an active session smoothly while working, without abrupt unexpected logouts during active typing or browsing.

#### Factor C: Core Technical Capabilities
- **TECH1 (Collar QR Scanner):** The hardware camera viewfinder and image file QR decoder resolved pet profiles quickly and accurately.
- **TECH1-B (Public Emergency Pet Passport):** Being able to scan an animal's collar and immediately view emergency medical information and security hotlines without forcing an immediate login is highly practical in emergencies.
- **TECH2 (AI Trait & Keyword Search):** The search accurately returned relevant animal profiles when searching with physical descriptors (e.g., "calico cat", "white socks") and handled exclusions appropriately.
- **TECH3 (Multi-Device Responsiveness):** The application adapted seamlessly between mobile phones and widescreen desktops without clipped cards, awkward overflows, or excessive dead white space.

### Section 5: Standardized Usability Scale (SUS — Quick 4-Item Benchmark)
*(Scored on a 5-point Likert Scale: 1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree)*

- **SUS-B1:** I think that I would like to use Task Force Bruno frequently for campus animal welfare. *(Positive Polarity)*
- **SUS-B2:** I found the system unnecessarily complex or difficult to understand. *(Negative Polarity)*
- **SUS-B3:** I felt very confident navigating and using the features of the system. *(Positive Polarity)*
- **SUS-B4:** I needed to learn too many things or ask for help before I could use the system effectively. *(Negative Polarity)*

---

### Section 6: Staff & Clinic Operational Modules (Staff Only)
*(Administered exclusively to Cohort B participants)*

- **STF1 (Sighting Intake Handoff):** The ability for staff to verify an animal sighting and directly admit it into pet registration (auto-filling location and photo) eliminates redundant manual data entry.
- **STF2 (Supply Dimensional Clarity):** Tracking inventory by genuine physical units (kilograms for kibble, cans for wet food, vials for vaccines, pieces for assets) provides clear and realistic supply management.
- **STF3 (Collar Tag Generation):** The integrated QR tag generator makes it easy for staff to create and print official, scannable collar tags for campus animals.

---

### Section 7: Behavioral Intention & Overall Satisfaction
- **SAT1 (Overall Satisfaction):** Overall, how satisfied are you with your experience using Task Force Bruno?
- **BI1 (Adoption Intention):** Assuming full campus rollout at CIT-U, I intend to actively use Task Force Bruno whenever I encounter stray animals, resident companions, or adoption opportunities.
- **BI2 (Endorsement / Recommendation):** I would enthusiastically recommend Task Force Bruno to fellow students, campus staff, and animal welfare advocates.

### Sections 8 & 9: Qualitative Diagnostics & Longitudinal Consent
- **Q15 (Encountered Issues & Friction Points):** *"If you experienced any confusing screen, visual glitch, camera access issue, or unexpected behavior, please describe where and what happened."*

- **Q17 (General Critique & Recommendations):** *"Share any additional thoughts or suggestions for the engineering team."*
